# Hardknock V0.21 end-of-pass report

V0.21 implements bounded multi-agent role, evidence, and authority controls. It is
not a swarm framework and makes no general claim that multiple agents outperform a
single agent.

## Architecture and persistence

1. Agent roles are custom records with five built-in templates.
2. Teams bind distinct member IDs to authenticated sessions and agent descriptors.
3. Role assignments have deterministic knowledge scope and bounded validity.
4. Delegated authority is the intersection of source, target role, team, runtime,
   and external grants; chains are at most two links.
5. Delegations are immutable, expiring, revocable, revision-bound, and optionally
   pinned to a plan revision.
6. Responsibilities cover plan, step, composition step, claim, experiment, effect,
   recovery, and review subjects with one owner per revision.
7. Contributions preserve proposal/observation/hypothesis/challenge/experiment/
   review/execution/recovery epistemic types.
8. Review findings are structured and immutable; local dispositions retain both
   finding and resolution.
9. High-risk review separates proposer, reviewer, and executor.
10. Schema 26 stores teams/delegations, 27 reviews/findings, 28 handoffs, and 29
    immutable governance, team records, and effect actors.

## Knowledge, evidence, and challenge

11. `RoleKnowledgePolicy` provides full, role-scoped, blind-challenge, and minimal
    exposure modes.
12. Blind delivery removes selected dominant experience while retaining constraints
    and anti-pattern warnings.
13. Delivered views are recorded and checked at challenge completion.
14. Member dependency profiles cover model, prompt, retrieval, experience, tool,
    evaluator, environment, federation, and external evidence metadata.
15. Declared profiles create common-mode warnings but never evidence or diversity.
16. `TeamEpistemicProfile` reuses canonical paths, diversity, fault domains, and
    dependency analysis.
17. Team fusion is evidence-based; agent counts and votes are ignored.
18. Challenge assignments bind role, strategy, review, expiry, evidence, tokens,
    and logical latency.
19. Challenge yield requires new canonical paths; relays and prose do not count.
20. Alternative strategies must change their named dependency dimension; controlled
    strategies require recorded multi-candidate experiment results.

## Runtime, effects, plans, and recovery

21. Runtime resolves authenticated role/delegation context and rejects omitted team
    context for registered sessions.
22. Bridge execution now applies team checks even when no knowledge hierarchy exists.
23. Caller-supplied team assessments are discarded and recomputed.
24. Denied published decisions record a `RoleViolation`; repetition can produce a
    non-promoting guard recommendation.
25. Concrete capability requirements remain required in addition to role authority.
26. High-risk review, plan validity, composition validity, and team authority are
    rechecked at decision publication.
27. Plan steps can bind responsible roles and executing members.
28. Composition steps use immutable responsibility assignments.
29. Plan revisions invalidate pinned delegations.
30. Structured handoffs retain sender/recipient role, review, evidence IDs, snapshot,
    expiry, and content hash without transferring raw scratchpads.
31. Sensitive/restricted handoffs fail closed pending a dedicated disclosure adapter.
32. Recovery actions require a validated Recovery and an explicit handoff containing
    failure evidence, plan state, and receipts for referenced committed effects.
33. Recovery does not inherit Executor capabilities.
34. Role reassignment requires evidence, a knowledge snapshot, target capability
    coverage, and an atomic team/governance revision.
35. Effect commit and reconciliation revalidate team authority at TOCTOU.
36. `EffectActorContext` retains agent, member, role, delegation chain, team, runtime
    decision, plan/step, snapshot, review, and authority source atomically with receipt.
37. Agent self-approval remains impossible; explicit external commit authorization wins.

## Economics, assurance, protocol, and CLI

38. Economics adds diversity, delegation, common-mode, and role-separation opportunities.
39. Formation recommends zero additional agents when current diverse evidence suffices.
40. Curriculum adds five team-boundary goals and accepts only bounded isolated local trials.
41. `team-assurance-basic-v1` evaluates explicit roles, capability scope, review
    separation, handoff integrity, and dependency metadata.
42. `team-epistemic-diversity-v1` additionally requires moderate diversity and a
    completed challenge.
43. Assurance is scoped to an action/team revision and never certifies a team as safe.
44. Bridge DTOs cover role, delegation, handoff, finding, challenge, and decision context.
45. Administrative mutations remain local CLI operations, not agent self-service.
46. `team why` renders responsibilities, challenges, dependencies, violations,
    guard recommendations, and immutable history.

## Mandatory fixtures and benchmark

47. The mandatory cases are covered by named tests for correlated reviewers, blind
    delivery, alternative evaluators, authority separation, self-approval,
    delegation escalation/expiry/scope, secret and structured handoffs, handoff/root
    provenance, low/high-risk separation, Recovery scope/escalation, plan revision,
    reviewer contamination, reassignment, and redundant-run avoidance.
48. The three-arm fixture reports Single Agent failure, Naive Majority 3/3 correlated
    failure, and Role-Separated success after one contradiction and two controlled paths.
49. Authority and effect bypass escapes are zero in the role-separated arm.
50. The local unoptimized authority microbenchmark measured 10,000 validations in
    70,864,083 ns (7,086 ns/op) with no external model invocation.

## Limits and deviations

51. Dependency metadata is observable/declarative, not proof of statistical independence.
52. The comparative result is fixture-local; logical latency counts workflow stages,
    and the microbenchmark is one local debug-build observation.
53. Sensitive disclosure, remote authentication/signatures, general DLP, dynamic agent
    spawning, voting governance, global scheduling, and distributed synchronization
    remain outside V0.21. V0.22 owns cross-environment synchronization and local trust.

Machine-readable fixture results are in `v0.21-benchmark.json`. Detailed semantics
are in `team-review.md`, `team-handoffs.md`, and `team-governance.md`.
